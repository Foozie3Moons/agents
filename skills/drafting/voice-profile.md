# Voice profile

How Foozie's written output sounds. Plain-spoken engineer talking to a peer.

## Sentences and rhythm

- Normal capitalization, complete sentences, contractions.
- Short to medium sentences. A longer explanatory sentence is often followed by a short punchline: "That's still on you." "It was terrible."
- Rhetorical questions are rare and only used as setup for a point, never as decoration.
- Casual shorthand is fine in informal channels: "x", "vs", "a bit", "honestly".
- Spoken-register fillers ("lol", "basically", "more or less") show up in his dictation, not in his written output. Do not carry them from dictation into a draft.

## Stance

- Opens from the problem or from his own weakness, never from a hook. An intro that reads like it was "shot out of a gun" gets rejected.
- Self-deprecating over self-promoting. Words like "visionary" get cut. "I had to get used to it unfortunately" stays.
- Claims are scoped. He concedes a point and then adds the catch: "Running and maintaining it has gotten easier too. The catch is it's on you now instead of the vendor."
- Specific over general. "Weeks took a weekend" became "months, a day to get a working version, a few days of polish at most."
- Accountability framing recurs: his name is on it, so he wants guardrails.
- Pushback is direct and short. No cushioning before a disagreement.

## Structure by channel

- **Slack, LinkedIn, Discord:** two or three short paragraphs, no headers. Slack status updates use bold labels followed by a colon, backticked identifiers, no nested bold.
- **MR descriptions:** see `checklists/mr-description.md`.
- **Blog:** first-person narrative arc, sections, concrete tooling details, an explicit downsides section.

## Hard rules

- No em dashes in anything external. Use a period, a comma, a colon, or parentheses.
- No all-lowercase style.
- No banned vocabulary. See `banned-words.md`.
- No AI tics. See `tics.md`.
- Minimal edits once content is set. Rewrites drift from his voice.
- Nothing attributed to him that he did not say. See `attribution.md`.
