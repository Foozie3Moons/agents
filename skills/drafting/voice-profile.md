# Voice profile

How Foozie's writing sounds. An engineer talking to a peer.

## Sentences and rhythm

- Normal capitalization, complete sentences, contractions.
- Short to medium sentences. He often follows a longer explanatory sentence with a short one: "That's still on you." "It was terrible."
- Rhetorical questions are rare. When he uses one it sets up a point.
- Casual shorthand is fine in informal channels: "x", "vs", "a bit", "honestly".
- Spoken-register fillers ("lol", "basically", "more or less") show up in his dictation, not in his written output. Do not carry them from dictation into a draft.

## Stance

- Opens from the problem or from his own weakness. He rejected an intro for starting "shot out of a gun".
- Self-deprecating rather than self-promoting. He cut "visionary" and kept "I had to get used to it unfortunately".
- Claims are scoped. He concedes a point and then adds the downside: "Running and maintaining it has gotten easier too. The catch is it's on you now instead of the vendor."
- Specific numbers and durations. He replaced "weeks took a weekend" with "months, a day to get a working version, a few days of polish at most."
- He comes back to accountability often: his name is on the work, so he wants guardrails.
- Pushback is direct and short, with nothing in front of it.

## Structure by channel

- **Slack, LinkedIn, Discord:** two or three short paragraphs, no headers. Slack status updates use bold labels followed by a colon, backticked identifiers, no nested bold.
- **MR descriptions:** see `checklists/mr-description.md`.
- **Blog:** first-person narrative arc, sections, concrete tooling details, an explicit downsides section.

## Hard rules

- No em dashes in anything external. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses.
- No all-lowercase style.
- No banned vocabulary. See `banned-words.md`.
- No AI tics. See `tics.md`.
- Minimal edits once the content is set. Rewrites lose his voice.
- Nothing attributed to him that he did not say. See `attribution.md`.
