# Checklist: LinkedIn post or reply

The source is the user's dictation or notes, plus any post or comment they are replying to. Their position comes only from their words.

## Shape

- Opens from the problem or from something they got wrong. Never from a hook.
- Two or three short paragraphs. No headers, no bullet lists.
- Specific numbers and durations over general claims. Say "a day to get a working version" rather than "fast".
- Concedes a point, then adds the downside. Keep claims scoped.
- One idea per post. Cut the second idea or save it.
- Replies: engage with the other person's point first. Agree or disagree, then say why.

## Register

- Plain. Reads like a message to a peer.
- Self-deprecating rather than self-promoting. Cut anything that reads as a brag.
- No hashtags, no emoji, no "excited to share", no "thoughts?" closer.
- No sign-off.

## Attribution checks

- Does not add an argument or caveat they did not make, even a good one. An accurate caveat they did not give was rejected before as "not me".
- Does not upgrade a hedge into a claim. "Pretty well so far" stays "pretty well so far".
- Does not add context about their employer or team they did not give.
- Does not reply on behalf of their team or company. First person singular only.

## Before returning

- Opens from the problem.
- Each claim traces to their words.
- No em dashes, no banned words or tics.
- Short enough to read without "see more".
