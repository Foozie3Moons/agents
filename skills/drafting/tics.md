# AI tics

Patterns that read as model-generated even when every word is allowed. Banned vocabulary is in `banned-words.md`. Check the draft against both.

Add a tic when you catch one. New model releases bring new ones.

## Sentence shapes

- "It's not X, it's Y." Also "not X but Y" and "less about X, more about Y" used as a reveal.
- Fragment triplets. Three short fragments in a row for rhythm. "Fast. Simple. Done."
- Tidy question sets. Two or three rhetorical questions in a row, then the answer.
- Parallel pairs with mirrored structure. "X does A; Y does B."
- A short tag sentence added for punch after the point was already made.
- Colon reveals. "The result: ..." "The catch: ..." as a recurring device. (A bold label with a colon in a Slack status update is a convention, not a tic.)

## Openers and closers

- Preamble that restates the ask before answering it.
- Enthusiasm openers: "Excited to share", "Thrilled to announce", "Quick update!"
- Summary closers: "In short", "At the end of the day", "Overall", "Ultimately".
- Call-to-action closers in posts: "Curious what others think", "Would love to hear your thoughts."
- Thanking the reader for reading.

## Structure

- Headers on short pieces.
- Bullet lists where two sentences of prose would do, especially in Slack and LinkedIn.
- Bold applied to the "key phrase" of each paragraph.
- Exactly three of everything: three examples, three reasons, three bullets.
- A balanced "on the other hand" paragraph added for symmetry when they only argued one side.

## Tone

- Hedging stacks: "I think this might possibly".
- Confident generality in place of a specific: "significant improvements" where they gave a number.
- Over-explaining a joke or a shorthand they used.
- Neutral tone on something they had an opinion about.
- Polishing out rough phrasing they dictated ("it was terrible", "unfortunately").

## Exemptions

Quoted text from other people and anything inside code spans or blockquotes is exempt.
